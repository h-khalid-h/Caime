# Security and privacy

What Caime protects, how, and what is not done yet. Each control names the test that proves it;
a control without a test is listed as a gap, not a control. Reviewed 2026-09-26.

## What matters most

1. **How you label someone is yours.** A relationship ("Manager · DATA C") is only ever sent to
   its owner unless they share it (ADR-10). Other code gets spheres to evaluate the owner's own
   privacy rules and never forwards them.
2. **Profile fields and read positions follow the owner's rules**, reciprocally for read
   receipts (R25), on every path: API views and live events.
3. **Strangers can't intrude.** Messages from people you're not connected with, or from an
   organization you haven't written to, are requests; they never notify, never create work, their
   links are inert until accepted, reading one never tells the sender, and a declined one stays
   shut while reading to its sender as unanswered (R14).
4. **Younger people are protected by rules in code** (R29): adults can't find under-18 accounts
   they don't know, email discovery is off, message requests are limited.

## Controls

| Area | Control | Proven by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passwords | scrypt with per-user salt. Unknown accounts and wrong passwords get the same error (tested), and a decoy hash keeps their timing alike (code, not measured). Asked for again while signed in (a new password, new recovery codes), it has ten tries in ten minutes, counted before anything else is done, so a session someone took can't be used to find it | `auth.test.ts` |
| Recovery codes | Ten per account, shown once, each good once, even tried twice at the same moment. Kept as slow scrypt hashes with a salt of the account's own, so a copy of the database can't be tried against every possible code, as it could against a fast hash of 40 random bits. One slow hash checks a try against all of an account's codes, and a try always makes one, so it takes about as long whether or not the account exists or has codes left (the hashes are counted in a test, not timed). A code can't be kept without its salt. Making new ones asks for the password again, and an account's codes are replaced one set at a time (its row is locked while they are), so two made at once leave ten codes with one salt | `auth.test.ts` |
| Sessions | Opaque random tokens, stored as SHA-256; revocable per device; password change and recovery revoke the others. A push goes only to a device whose session is still live, so one signed out from elsewhere gets no more notifications or previews. The app says whose account it's showing on every call (`X-Caime-User`, but for the few that find out or decide who's signed in), and a call that finds someone else signed in (another tab of the browser signed in as them since) is refused (`409 wrong_account`), so nothing queued or sent as one person ever goes as another; the realtime socket checks its hello the same way, and a tab follows another's sign-out or sign-in at once | `auth.test.ts`, `actions.test.ts`, `wrong-account.test.ts`, `e2e/core-flow.spec.ts` |

| Possible duplicates | Two of someone's people are offered as possibly one only from what that person sees of them: the name each shows them, their own nicknames and their own labels. Nobody blocked either way is offered: blocking takes back an offer already made, and one still standing isn't merged. | Groups | Who runs a group is the server's to say (PRD §56): its owner makes admins and members again; admins add people they're connected with and remove members; the owner removes admins; nobody removes the owner, and anyone may leave. An owner who leaves, or whose account is deleted, or who leaves the space a conversation is in, hands it to the admin who's been in it longest, else whoever has, never to an app's bot; each change is a line everyone in it sees, and those lines (who joined, who renamed it) are never deleted for everyone. Every change to who's in a group or who runs it is made under that group's own lock, reading who's who under it, so two at once never leave it with two owners or none. Someone who leaves is nobody in it any more: added again, they start as a member from then, not as the admin they were nor as its longest there, and what Caime had offered them about it goes too. A group's name, what it's for, its context and disappearing messages change only by its owner and admins (either person in a one-to-one), and so does its context through `/contexts`; a decision changes only by whoever recorded or made it, or the group's owner and admins. A context is linked to a conversation only by someone who may change it, so an id alone opens nothing and a member can't take a group's context to a one-to-one of their own to change it there; its maker keeps it only while it's linked to nothing; a task or decision points back only to a message in the conversation it's added in and a context its maker can see, so none quotes a message from anywhere else. Someone out of a group finds none of its decisions in search. A mention tells only someone in the conversation, and a private conversation has none (its server can't read who a message names). A group's owner and admins delete anyone's message for everyone, and they (or either person in a one-to-one) pin messages at its top, five at most and never between a customer and an organization, nor by anyone blocked from writing there, nor while a message request in it is unanswered (it allows one message and nothing more); a message deleted for everyone is pinned for nobody, and one deleted for oneself leaves one's own view of the top at once (still counted in the five, and the refusal says so). A change to a message reaches everyone as its changer sees it, so each device keeps its own votes and works out its own reactions, and changes only a message it already shows: none is added to anyone's history by it, nor brought back after being deleted for oneself. A shared file, link or date from a message deleted for oneself is left out of what one is shown of the conversation. A group's topics have exactly its people in the roles they have in it: every change to who's in the group or who runs it is made to its topics under the same lock, so someone removed is out of every topic at once, nobody is added to or removed from a topic directly, and a topic is as private as its group, its messages disappear as the group's do (changed only in the group, and said in each topic), and someone coming into one has it as muted as the group. A topic starts only from someone who may write where it starts, from a one-to-one only between two people connected now and never a private one, never with an organization, and with the same rules whether it's started from the details or by accepting a suggestion; a block takes back any topic Caime had offered either of the two | `messaging.test.ts` (running a group, pinned messages), `actions.test.ts`, `mentions.test.ts`, `locale.test.ts`, E2E |
| Forwarding | A message is forwarded only from a conversation its sender is in, to conversations they're in and may write in, each checked first with everything sending there would check (blocks, a closed business conversation, a message request that has had its one message), so it goes to all of them or none, and each copy counts toward the sender's rate limit; the app offers none this device has seen as private, whatever the server says now; never from or into a private conversation; cards, polls, live locations and lines about a conversation stay where they were shared, so no copy carries another card's state or someone's whereabouts | `messaging.test.ts` (forwarding), `private.test.ts` |
| Automations and what's saved | An automation acts only for whoever set it up, on what arrives for them in conversations they're in: never on a private conversation (sealed; nothing in it is read or saved), never on a message request they haven't accepted, never on anything from someone blocked either way, and never on their own messages. In a conversation with an organization, to its team the other side is a customer and to the customer it's an organization: the team keeps only what the customer sends, never what a teammate, its AI agent or a bot does, and the customer keeps what the organization sends whoever on it wrote, their own blocks of people on its team included, so what's missing never says who that was; what a customer saved names the organization, never who on its team sent it. What's saved points at the message (or one file or link of it) and copies nothing: deleted for everyone, disappeared or deleted for oneself, it's gone from what anyone saved (and from their lists on every open device), and a conversation someone has left shows nothing of it in what they saved. Saving by hand is refused in a private conversation and from a request not yet accepted. Each person's automations (50) and saved items (5,000, counting only what they can see and remove) are capped, each counted and added one at a time for that person so two at once never pass the cap, saving is rate-limited, and nobody reads, changes or removes another's automations or saved items. Both are in the person's export: what each automation does, and where each saved thing is, never the words of someone else's message | `automations.test.ts`, `automations.test.ts` (core), E2E |
| Blocking | Blocked people can't message or find you; a profile of someone who blocked you looks like it doesn't exist. A block stops every way of reaching the other side in your conversation, not only sending: editing, reacting, voting, moving a card and typing all ask one check first (`lib/blocks.ts`), both ways, and neither can start a new conversation with the other from a topic Caime offered before. A customer can block an organization: its team and its apps' bots can no longer write to them, the conversation closes (resolved for the team, "Closed by the customer"; archived for the customer), and they can't be written to again until they unblock it; nobody on a team can block their own organization | `connections.test.ts`, `actions.test.ts`, `blocking.test.ts`, `e2e/around-the-conversation.spec.ts` |
| Minors | Discoverability, email and message-request rules. With organizations: they write only to verified ones, which never write to them first; its team is told they're under 18, and no card about money (an Invoice, an Order, a Purchase order, a Payment request, an organization's own card with an amount) reaches them | `privacy-safety.test.ts`, `auth.test.ts`, `connections.test.ts`, `business-start.test.ts`, `business-minors.test.ts` |
| Kit cards | The server decides what a card says and where it starts; a client's `state` or extra fields are dropped. A card moves only along its kit's flow, by the person each move belongs to, and only if nobody moved it first. Money cards (invoice, purchase order, payment request) never involve anyone under 18. A checklist changes one step at a time under a row lock, so simultaneous ticks never undo each other; anyone in the conversation ticks and adds, only whoever added an item or made the list removes it. With an organization, only the cards a customer relationship offers are accepted. Locations are shared on purpose: once, or live for a time the sharer chose (15 minutes to 8 hours, kept by the server), moved only by the sharer, keeping only the latest point, and ended by them at any time; never by anyone under 18 (R29, refused by the server), and never live with an organization. An album takes only photos and videos the person adding them uploaded, never a file they were merely shown; whoever added a photo, or made the album, takes it out; nobody adds to a closed album | `kits.test.ts`, `checklists.test.ts`, `business.test.ts`, `albums.test.ts`, `live-location.test.ts` |
| Organizations writing first | Only a verified organization, and only a person on its team (never an app), writes to someone first, found by a handle they can be found by. Never to anyone under 18, anyone who blocked it or the writer, or anyone who only takes messages from people they know; any of those reads the same as nobody by that handle. A plan's starts a day limit it, and a customer writing first is never counted | `business-start.test.ts`, `messaging.test.ts`, E2E |
| Personal access tokens | A person's own, for their own scripts: named, with the permissions they chose (read or send messages, read or change actions, see their name and handle) and a lifetime, shown once and kept as a hash. A message sent with one says "via" its name | `personal-tokens.test.ts`, E2E |
| Calendar feed | A secret address (PRD §72) a calendar app reads with no sign-in: 32 random bytes, shown once as it's made and kept only as its SHA-256, so it can't be shown again, and never written to the server's log (its path is masked there). A new one ends the old at once, stopping it ends it, recovering the account ends it (whoever had the account may have made it), and deleting the account ends it with the account. Only a signed-in adult makes or stops one (no token reaches those routes); each is in the audit log. It holds only what's theirs to see: open actions of their own or asked of them (never someone's private waiting item), and meetings and appointments agreed in conversations they're still in, never from a private conversation, a message request they haven't accepted or a message they deleted for themselves. Titles are escaped so nothing in one can add a line to the calendar, and only an http(s) link is written as one. Reads are limited per address, lookups of addresses that don't exist per network address, and the answer is never cached | `calendar.test.ts`, `ics.test.ts`, E2E |
| Private conversations | End to end encrypted (R18, PRD §61), on the web and in the phone apps. A message is sealed on the sender's device: encrypted once with a fresh AES-256-GCM key, which is wrapped for every device of everyone in the conversation (the sender's own included) through an ephemeral P-256 ECDH exchange and HKDF, and the whole envelope is signed by the sending device (ECDSA P-256). The conversation, the sender's id for the message, which edit it is, the device and whose device it is are bound into both the encryption and the signature, so the server, which keeps only the envelope, can't read a message, alter or forge one, move one to another conversation or edit, or pass it off as another person's or another device's. A message is taken only sealed for exactly the live devices of everyone in it (a device lives as long as the session that registered it), from one of the sender's own devices; anything in plaintext is refused, and the server has no words to put in a notification, a reply preview, search, memory or AI. Its messages are never forwarded, and never written by a token or an app. Each device's private keys are made non-extractable in the browser and never leave it; on a phone, where the app encrypts in JavaScript (audited @noble libraries, byte for byte what Web Crypto makes and reads), they are 32 bytes each kept in the iOS Keychain or Android Keystore, readable after the phone's first unlock and never copied to a backup or another phone, and a failed read never makes new ones; signing out of a browser deletes them, and everything it kept of the account's private conversations (a phone keeps them, R41). Which devices are whose is never taken from the server: a person's first device vouches for itself, each later one waits until one of theirs approves it ("Is this you?", signed by the approving device), nothing is sealed for one still waiting, and each device checks every chain of approvals itself and pins every device's keys the first time it confirms them, so a later answer can't swap them. A device listed for someone that their chain doesn't vouch for is never sealed for, nor believed as a sender, and is shown. A person's security code is a digest of their chain's first device, the same as they add devices; it changes only when they start over (which retires their other devices), and then, for someone whose code was compared, nothing is sealed for them or shown from them until it's compared again. A message opens only in the conversation shown, only as its newest edit and only once (an old edit or a copy under another message isn't shown), and what it answers is sealed inside it. A conversation seen as private on a device is never written to in the clear from it, whatever the server says later, and in one nothing unsealed is shown. What's being written, and what waits to be sent, stays in memory: never mirrored to the account, never written to the browser's storage. Links in an opened message are checked on the device. A deleted account's devices keep their public keys (without names), so what it sent can still be checked. Removing a device signs it out. Someone blocked sees nothing of the other's devices. A private group holds up to 64 people. A device added later can't read what was sent before it, unless the person typed their recovery key on it (R41): the key, 160 random bits shown once and kept nowhere by Caime, stands for a recovery device the person's own approved device introduces into their chain, which every private message is then sealed for too (the server sees one more device, never signed in, never a sender); typed on a new device, its keys are derived there, that device reads what was sealed since the key was made, and is introduced by the recovery device, so the person's chain and code are unchanged; a wrong key derives another device and opens nothing; the key and the password together read everything, which the app says. A phone keeps its keys when its account signs out and takes its device up again on signing in (the server binds the same device, with the very keys, to the new session; a device removed elsewhere, or retired by starting over, registers afresh); a browser lets them go. What the server still sees: who is in it, when messages are sent and how long they are, and reactions. Only between connections | `e2ee.test.ts`, `e2ee-noble.test.ts` and `e2ee-recovery.test.ts` (core: every pairing of browser and phone), `private.test.ts` (server and app), `e2e/around-the-conversation.spec.ts` |
| Calls | 1:1 voice and video in a direct conversation, only between two people who can already write to each other: never from a message request that wasn't accepted, never with someone blocked, never in a group or business conversation. The server rings and relays; the media goes device to device, encrypted by WebRTC (DTLS-SRTP), and never through Caime. Of the person's devices, only the one that answered is in the call: offers, answers and candidates pass only between the caller's device and that one, so no other tab, device or person can join. One call at a time on each side; an unanswered call is missed after 45 seconds, and each side's device says for itself that it's still there, so a call either side left ends within 90 seconds and one side alone can't keep the other "busy". Blocking ends any call between the two at once, without a missed-call notice. "On another call" is said only to someone who may see the person's online status (and not while they're invisible); to anyone else the call rings, as long as any call rings, and is missed like any unanswered call, while the person isn't rung and finds it among their missed calls. The ring's notification is read once it stops ringing, its push lasts only as long as it rings and goes to browsers only (the phone apps can't answer yet). A screen is shared only when the person picks one in the browser's own picker, and goes the way the camera does (device to device, encrypted); the two devices tell each other only whether a camera is on, a screen is shown and the microphone is muted, over a data channel between them that never touches the server. A relay's credentials are signed and expire after 12 hours. Starting calls and passing signals are rate-limited, offers and answers more tightly (20 a side in ten minutes); a large signal passes through the database for at most ten minutes. Group calls are in a group conversation of up to eight people, each device connected to every other: everyone who took part in the group is rung, except anyone blocked either way with whoever calls; nobody joins a call with someone they're blocked with, and someone under 18 is in one only with people they're connected with (R29: a call shows faces, and each device's network address to the others), neither rung by nor joined with anyone else. Offers, answers and candidates pass only between two devices joined in it, never to or from one that isn't; a device that stops saying it's there is taken out after 90 seconds; leaving or being removed from the group, or deleting the account, is leaving its call at once; a block in the middle of one takes out whoever blocked (or stops the ring of the one blocked when only they were rung), without a word about why. One call at a time for each person, 1:1 or group, for real: placing, answering, starting or joining a call checks and does it under that person's own lock, so two of their devices can't put them in two calls; one call on at a time in a conversation (a unique index, so two people starting one at once get the same call); at most eight in it. Every change to who's in a group call is made under that call's lock, so leaving, turning it down, being taken out or the sweep never decides on a call someone has just joined, and the sweep checks again that whoever it takes out is still gone. What anyone in the group sees of a call is who's in it and their own place in it, never who else was rung, turned it down or missed it, nor whether anyone still is (which would tell the group who was left out by a block or R29, or who was around and said no): a ring turned down, run out or stopped is told only to whoever it was for, and changes nothing anyone else can see or count; and a call nobody answers rings its whole 45 seconds however soon everyone says no, and ends the same way ("no answer") whoever was rung. Someone no longer in the group sees only their own place in it as they're taken out, and nothing of it after. Devices are shown only to those in the call, and a signal names whose device it's for and from, so someone using another's device id takes nothing from them; only the device in a call can leave it, so a tab left behind can't take someone out of the call they moved to. Starting or joining reads who may take part under the person's own lock, and a block, a removal from the group or a connection ended waits for any start or join under way before it takes someone out; whatever slips through is taken out at that device's next beat, and nothing it signals is passed on. Offers and answers are limited for each pair of devices (20 in ten minutes), so nobody can spend what someone has for the others; joining, turning down and leaving are paced, and one that changes nothing tells nobody. Removing a connection with someone under 18 takes whoever removed it out of any call they share, as a block does. A missed group call reaches no phone of anyone who muted the group. Names in a call, its ring and its missed-call notice are as each person shows themselves to whoever sees them (their identity for that connection), 1:1 calls too. The record keeps who, when, how long and how it ended, never what was said. In call history, someone no longer in a group sees only their own place in its calls (not its name, who else was there, or how long it went on after them), and a group call lists only who was there once the person was part of it, the same for finding calls with someone | `calls.test.ts` (server and core), `group-calls.test.ts`, `call-history.test.ts`, E2E |
| AI agents | An organization's owner or admins turn on its AI agent, name it, and tell it what it answers from; every change is in the audit log. It is its own member of the team (kind `agent`): it can't sign in, be found, be written to first or be given a conversation, and everything it writes says "AI agent", in the conversation, in notifications and on the organization's page before anyone writes. It reads the conversation's last 20 messages labelled by who wrote them (Customer, Team, You), never names or addresses, and what the organization told it; `ai_runs` records the call and the conversation's id, never the text. What it can do is write one message, hand the conversation to the team, or close it; the prompt treats the conversation and the organization's text as information, never instructions. It never answers anyone under 18, a private conversation (R18), one a person on the team is in (who wrote since it was last resolved, or took it since the customer came back), one escalated, or one it handed over (until it's resolved); it deals with each customer message once, even with two workers on it. It answers at most ten times and calls the model at most twenty times in a conversation a day, answered or not, then hands it over and tells the customer so; an answer thrown away (the customer wrote again meanwhile) isn't counted against the plan, and it stops at what the plan includes. Tried before it's on, nothing is sent, and a try is one of the person's own AI assists | `agents.test.ts`, E2E |
| OAuth apps | A developer registers an app with exact return addresses (https, http only on localhost, or the app's own scheme; never `javascript:`, `data:` or `file:`, never a fragment or a password in it). An app's request is checked before anyone is asked: an unknown app, an address it didn't register, or a permission that doesn't exist is shown as such and sends them nowhere, so Caime never redirects to an address it wasn't given. Consent names the app, who made it and what it asks, needs a click (a cookie POST with the CSRF header; the page can't be framed), and nobody under 18 gives it. Authorization codes are single-use, last ten minutes, and are bound to their app, return address and PKCE challenge (S256 only); a code used twice ends what it gave. A code replayed after it expired is refused without ending anything. Access tokens last an hour; refresh tokens a month and turn over on every use, and one used twice ends the grant for both copies. A confidential app's secret is compared as a hash in constant time. Tokens act only within what the person allowed, never beyond their grant, on the same routes as personal tokens; allowing an app again only adds to what it may do (an app asking for less keeps what it had), and the person takes it all back by removing the app. A message sent says "via" the app (to a business customer, "via an app", never its name). The person removes an app at any time, and recovering the account removes them all; its developer removing it ends it for everyone; an app gives back only its own tokens. Only the token and revocation endpoints take form posts, so no other site can post a form to Caime (a login, say); they and the discovery document answer any site without credentials, so a browser app can trade its code. Codes, secrets and tokens are kept as hashes; allowing and removing are in the audit log | `oauth.test.ts`, `business.test.ts`, `access.test.ts`, E2E |

| Invite links | A link (R1) is a 128-bit token nobody guesses (`/i/<token>`, `invites.token`), made by someone signed in (thirty a day), good for thirty days for anyone who has it, and taken back by its maker at any time; a spent one is forgotten a month later. A block either way refuses; one's own link answers 400; someone already connected is sent to the conversation and counted again by nothing. ## Gaps

These are known and tracked in `docs/ROADMAP.md`. None is hidden behind a feature flag.

- **Standard conversations aren't end to end encrypted.** Their messages are protected by access
  control and the database's disk encryption. Private conversations (R18) are, on the web and in the phone
  apps (these not yet tried on a phone itself), and a private conversation's files, voice notes
  and cards aren't built: it takes text.
- **AI assist reads other people's messages** when someone asks it to catch up or find
  follow-ups in a standard conversation; they aren't asked first. A private conversation is the
  answer: AI never reads one. A message can also try to steer what the model writes; what it
  writes only ever reaches the person who asked, as a suggestion.
- **Public pages show only what's public.** A visitor who isn't signed in reads the landing
  page, an organization's profile, and a person's page only while that person can be found by
  handle (their switch) and is 18 or over, with only the fields whose audience is everyone
  (R44, `lib/public-pages.ts`, the privacy evaluator with nobody as the viewer). An
  organization's logo, and a photo its person shows to everyone, are served without sign-in for
  link cards; a person's photo otherwise still needs the viewer's relation. Everything a person
  wrote is escaped, and JSON-LD escapes `<`, so nothing on a page can close its script. The
  app's own screens are marked `noindex`; people are never listed in the sitemap.
- **Backups stay on the box.** A daily `pg_dump`, checked and kept 30 days (`docs/DEPLOY.md`,
  "Backups"), sits on the data volume beside uploads; uploads themselves aren't dumped. A copy off
  the host (the volume's own backup, or S3) is what a lost host needs, and isn't automated yet.
- **Moderation tooling.** Reports are reviewed at `/admin/reports` (R49): status, the message
  removed for everyone, an update taken back, the person suspended and the suspension lifted.
  The reporter is told once that it was looked at, and whether Caime acted, nothing more.
- **Rate limits are per instance** (in memory). With several instances behind a load balancer,
  limits multiply by the instance count until a shared store is added.
- **Email verification and password reset by email** need an email provider; recovery codes are
  the only reset path today, by design and documented to users.
- **`TRUST_PROXY` is on by default** for EasyPanel's proxy. Run without a proxy and clients can
  choose the address rate limits see; set `TRUST_PROXY=false` there.
- **No penetration test** by a third party yet.
- **Reserved handles are matched by their letters.** A lookalike, with a digit or other letters
  standing in for the product's (ca1me, cairne), isn't refused. Anyone who took a handle before it
  was reserved keeps it, and the operator can't give it out while they do.
- **A held handle is held from whoever had it, too.** Caime keeps no record of whose it was, so
  someone who wants back a handle they changed from, or who deletes their account and returns,
  gets it within the year only from the operator, once they've shown it was theirs. A link to an
  old handle doesn't follow its person to their new one: it finds nobody. Handles let go of before
  the hold existed (0037) weren't held.
- **Webhooks carry what customers write** to the organization's own endpoint, which is the
  point of them, and the organization then holds that copy under its own policies. Customers
  see that the organization answers through an app ("Automated"), not where its webhooks go.
- **Plans are set by hand.** Until billing is connected (⛔ Stripe), the operator changes plans
  with one shared token; whoever holds it can change anyone's plan. Keep it in the environment
  settings, never in chat, and rotate it by changing the variable.
- **Calls show each side the other's network address.** Media goes device to device, so, as with
  any direct WebRTC call, each device learns the other's IP address. A relay (TURN) could carry
  every call to hide it; Caime doesn't force that. Without a relay configured, calls on strict
  networks don't connect at all (⛔ TURN).
- **An AI agent can be wrong.** It answers only from what its organization told it, and says it's
  an AI in every message, but a model can misread a question or the organization's text. The
  organization answers for what it tells it; customers can always ask for a person, and the team
  sees every conversation it answered.
- **Apps aren't reviewed.** Anyone over 18 can register an app, and people can let it in the
  moment it's registered. The consent screen names its developer by their Caime name and handle
  and shows its website as they typed it, unverified; what an app may do is limited to the
  permissions people allow, never their account.
- **Webhook deliveries share each instance's job queue.** An endpoint that is slow to answer
  holds one of eight slots for up to 10 seconds per attempt, so a very busy organization with a
  slow endpoint can delay other organizations' deliveries until its attempts run out.
