# Caime, reviewed: 2026-09-30

The whole product looked at before the next block of work: what's there, where it stands against
the alternatives (docs/COMPETITIVE.md), and what to do first. Facts come from the repository,
production (https://caime.datac.com) and its services; nothing here is estimated. Refinements
that follow from it are numbered in docs/PRODUCT-REVIEW.md; the plan is in docs/ROADMAP.md (M9).

## In one paragraph

Caime's wedge, relationship-aware 1:1 communication, is built and verified end to end on the web:
connect in three taps, the relationship drives attention, notifications, rules, search, memory and
AI assist, and organizations, spaces, calls, private conversations and billing sit on it. What
stops it outperforming the alternatives today is not what it does but where and how it's reached:
it is a web product (no app in a store, phone push and calls unverified on a device), a visitor
who isn't signed in sees an empty shell (no landing page, no public profile a search engine or an
answer engine can read, no sitemap), the database has no backups, and the paid tiers sell capacity
(AI, storage, seats) rather than the wedge. Those, in that order, are the plan.

## Findings by dimension

Severity: **P0** hurts people or the business now; **P1** blocks winning against an alternative;
**P2** quality and polish.

### Reach and discovery (web, SEO, AEO)

- **P0** No backups of the production database. EasyPanel's `db` service has no backup
  configured (checked through its API on 2026-09-30) and docs/DEPLOY.md never mentions pg_dump,
  restore or point-in-time recovery. One bad deploy or disk loses every conversation.
- **P1** A signed-out visitor to `/`, `/@handle`, `/o/handle` and any unknown path gets the same
  1.9 KB app shell (HTTP 200, `<title>Caime</title>`, one meta description, no Open Graph or
  Twitter card, no canonical, no JSON-LD). Nothing about Caime, a person or an organization is
  readable without JavaScript. Search engines index an empty page; answer engines have nothing to
  quote; a shared `cai.me/@handle` link unfurls blank in WhatsApp, iMessage and Slack, which is
  where Caime must win (COMPETITIVE §5, §8). Unknown paths answer 200 (soft 404).
- **P1** No `sitemap.xml` (404). `robots.txt` is Cloudflare's managed content-signals text with
  no rules of Caime's and no `Sitemap:` line.
- Good: Brotli on HTML and JS, hashed assets `immutable` for a year, HTML `no-cache`, HSTS,
  CSP without `unsafe-eval`, nosniff, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`,
  a web manifest with `theme-color`. Missing: `Permissions-Policy`.
- Web JS budget: 449.4 KB gzip of 450 (scripts/bundle-budget.mjs), so the next feature on the
  first screen must pay for itself; no other performance budget has a recorded value.

### Product and the wedge

- The wedge works end to end on the web (ROADMAP M1–M6 ticks: connect flow, policy engine,
  attention inbox, tasks/waiting/decisions, rules, relationship profile, search, AI assist).
- **P1** It is not in anyone's pocket: no EAS configuration or store build (⛔ Apple and Google
  developer accounts, `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN`), native bundles never run on a device, Expo push
  unverified, calls ring only while the app is open, private conversations on phones untried.
  The wedge's main payoff, relationship-aware notifications, reaches phones only through Web Push.
- **P1** No network bootstrap: importing exported WhatsApp chats is "later" (COMPETITIVE §5) and
  not on the roadmap; `cai.me` is still parked at Afternic, which also holds `cai.me/@handle`
  (R35) and passkeys (R36).
- **P2** Standard conversations are not end to end encrypted (R18 keeps that per conversation,
  by design); AI assist reads the other person's messages without asking them (SECURITY gaps).
  Against Signal this is a stated trade, not a bug, and the privacy page must keep saying so.

### Monetization

- Plans (packages/core/src/plans.ts): Personal free (10 AI/day, 5 GB), Pro (200 AI/day, 100 GB),
  organizations Free (3 people, 1 app, 20 starts/day, 50 agent replies) and Business (100 people,
  25 apps, insights, 1,000 starts, 2,000 agent replies), Enterprise above. Live prices exist on
  the Stripe account (Pro €6/€60, Business €29/€290; lookup keys `caishy_*`); the ROADMAP line
  saying they still need setting up is stale and corrected in this block.
- **P1** The paid tiers sell capacity, not the wedge: PRD §84 promises Pro "advanced relationship
  rules" and "multi-identity", and plans.ts gates neither. R23 says the wedge is never counted,
  which is right; but Pro then needs something relationship-shaped of its own (see plan).
- "Get Business" on production failed until 2026-09-29 with a hidden Stripe refusal; the refusal
  is now shown in Stripe's words. Its cause is still to be read from the next attempt.

### Engineering, reliability, scale

- Multi-instance ready in the main: realtime fan-out over Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY, jobs claimed
  with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, migrations under an advisory lock with checksums, graceful
  shutdown, `/v1/healthz` and `/v1/readyz`, request ids on every log line, hot paths indexed.
- **P1** What breaks with two instances: the in-memory rate limiter (limits multiply), uploads on
  a local `/data` volume until the S3 adapter lands, periodic tasks on every worker unless
  `WORKERS=false`. NOTIFY is serialized per database, so fan-out caps throughput before
  broadcasts (R40) can be measured at 500,000.
- **P1** No alerting and no external uptime check are documented; `/metrics` has no job-queue
  depth, oldest-job age or bus-lag gauge, so a stuck worker is invisible.
- **P2** No dependency audit in CI (no `pnpm audit`, Dependabot or Renovate). No down migrations
  (acceptable with backups and forward fixes; unacceptable without backups).
- Security posture is sound for the size: scrypt passwords, opaque hashed session tokens, httpOnly
  cookie plus `X-Caime-Client` for CSRF, CORS by allow-list, sniffed and re-encoded uploads,
  SSRF-safe webhooks including on resolved addresses. Open per SECURITY.md: third-party pentest,
  moderation reviewer UI, email verification and email password reset, one shared admin token,
  `TRUST_PROXY` on by default, apps unreviewed.

### UX and UI

- First run: welcome, sign-up (name, handle, email, password, birth date, country), then three
  onboarding steps (recovery codes, how rules work, find or share). No dead end: "Later" lands on
  Chats with "Find people". **P2** Onboarding explains the rules but never walks the first
  connection through to a first message, which is the wedge's moment (PRD §87).
- **P2** Empty states with no next action: Requests (no way to share your link), Notifications,
  call history, Updates ("Follow an organization" with no way to find one), Actions (no "Add"),
  the Business inbox.
- **P2** Destructive actions on one tap, no confirmation or undo: removing a connected app,
  revoking a token, blocking an organization from a request banner, taking back an update.
- **P2** Loading: Notifications and Requests render nothing until loaded (no skeleton, no retry);
  Saved shows "Loading…" text instead of the skeleton. About a hundred places toast the server's
  message as it is; that's fine while every server message is written for people, which they are.
- **P2** Copy: "them" as a fallback in call history breaks the names-not-pronouns rule (R27).
- **P2** Theme: 19 raw hex colours outside theme and brand (white on fills is allowed by
  convention 9; `#FFE3EC`, `#FFD6E7`, `#0B0B12`, `#E5484D`, `#30A46C` are not). Four pressables
  without an accessibility label (toast action, stop sharing location, a task row, one in the
  context panel). About 57 physical left/right styles remain where RTL needs start/end; the
  onboarding uses `align="right"`.
- The PRD's §63 five tabs became four plus You in the header, and the desktop context panel
  closes with an X without remembering it: both deliberate (PRODUCT-REVIEW R7–R9), kept.

### Web presence, in detail

- The export is a single-page app (`web.output: single`): the server sends the same shell for
  every HTML request; signed-out routing happens in the browser (`Stack.Protected`), so
  `/@handle` shows "Create an account or sign in to see @x" and nothing is public. The only
  server-rendered pages are `/privacy`, `/terms` and `/help` (`lib/pages.ts`), with a title and
  description but no Open Graph, canonical or JSON-LD.
- Only the calendar feed carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`; auth and app screens are not marked.
- Fonts (Nunito and Inter, eight weights) load from JavaScript with a 2.5 s fallback, so the
  first paint waits on the bundle; no preload or preconnect.
- Compression is the proxy's (Brotli observed), not the server's; if the proxy changes, it goes.
- Manifest lacks `id`, `screenshots`, `shortcuts`, `categories` and `lang`. The service worker
  keeps the shell and built files for offline opening and never caches API responses (right).

## The plan (M9 in docs/ROADMAP.md)

Ordered by what each unblocks, cheapest first within a tier.

1. **P0 Backups.** Nightly `pg_dump` of the production database to object storage with 30 days
   kept, a documented restore drill, and a `/metrics` gauge for the last backup's age. Blocks
   nothing else; nothing else is safe without it.
2. **P1 A readable web.** A server-rendered landing page at `/` for visitors who aren't signed in
   (what Caime is, for whom, the wedge in one screen, sign in / start), and server-rendered public
   profiles at `/@handle` and `/o/handle` with title, description, Open Graph and Twitter cards,
   canonical, JSON-LD (`Person`, `Organization`), a real 404, `sitemap.xml` for organizations'
   public pages, a `robots.txt` of Caime's own, and `Permissions-Policy`. The app takes over the
   moment JavaScript runs, so nothing changes for people signed in. Only what the person or
   organization already shows to everyone is rendered (ADR-10, R2); a person can turn their
   public page off.
3. **P1 Operations.** An uptime check on `/v1/readyz` and alert rules on it, on 5xx rate, on
   job-queue depth and oldest-job age (new gauges), and on backup age; `pnpm audit` in CI.
4. **P1 The phone.** EAS configuration and a development build tried on the owner's iPhone
   (calls, private conversations, push), then TestFlight and Play internal testing. ⛔ Apple and
   Google developer accounts and `EXPO_ACCESS_TOKEN`: the owner's to provide; everything up to
   the accounts is built now.
5. **P1 Pro that sells the wedge.** Give Pro what PRD §84 promised and R23 allows: unlimited
   custom rules (Personal keeps a few), several identities, relationship history beyond a year,
   and the relationship analytics of §71 for a person; keep every core relationship feature free.
   Decide with the owner before building: it changes what Personal loses.
6. **P1 Network bootstrap.** Import of an exported WhatsApp chat into a conversation with the
   right person (text and media, dates kept), so the history comes along on day one; then the
   contextual invite measured at ≤ 60 s invite-to-reply in E2E as COMPETITIVE §5 asks.
7. **P2 Polish that shows.** Every empty state ends in an action; confirmations (or undo) on
   the four one-tap destructive actions; skeletons and retry on Requests and Notifications; the
   pronoun fallback gone; the raw colours through the theme; the four missing labels; start/end
   instead of left/right where RTL reads them; onboarding that walks the first connection to a
   first message.
8. **P2** Rate limiter and caches in Postgres (or Redis) for the second instance; S3 adapter;
   moderation reviewer UI; email verification and reset; per-operator admin tokens; pentest.

What this review did not do: run a load test, a Lighthouse audit or an accessibility audit with a
screen reader on a device. Those are listed under M9 as measurements to take, not assumed.
